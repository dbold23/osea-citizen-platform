§ I The Animal
Most living sharks have five gill slits. The sevengill is one of just two species worldwide carrying seven — a body plan unchanged for ~150 million years.

A relict among modern sharks.

Found from Tomales Bay to the southern tip of Argentina, the broadnose sevengill is a coastal apex predator: solitary, cryptic, and slow to mature. It hunts seals, smaller sharks, and bony fish; it returns to the same bays year after year.

The freckle pattern on each animal — dense across the head, sparse along the flanks — is unique. Once we have a clean photograph, the pattern is enough to recognise that individual on every subsequent encounter, for the rest of its life.

§ II How You Help

A photograph from the right angle is worth a season of fieldwork.

i

The right side of the head, in focus.

The freckle pattern is bilaterally distinct — the right side and the left are different individuals to our model. A clean profile of either is the gold standard.

ii

EXIF metadata kept intact.

Phones, cameras, and most apps embed the time and (with permission) the location. We read it directly — no typing required. Submissions without metadata are still accepted, just with manual entry.

iii

Anything you remember.

Group size, water clarity, behaviour, prior sightings of what you suspect is the same shark. The notes field exists for everything that doesn't fit a checkbox.
