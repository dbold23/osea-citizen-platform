§ I The Method

Three steps. The first is yours.

i

You submit a photograph.

Drop it in the form below, from a phone or laptop, with or without location data. Our system reads the EXIF for time and place, no typing required.

ii

The model reads its freckles.

An open-source detector finds the body, the head, and every spot, all in roughly two seconds. Your photograph is now a constellation we can compare against the catalogue.

iii

A scientist confirms the identity.

If the constellation matches a known individual, we add the encounter to that animal's record. If it's new, the catalogue grows by one. Either way, you've helped.

§ II Active Programs

A field guide to who we're tracking.

The platform was built for sevengill sharks. Its bones (detection, cataloguing, identification) are species-agnostic. New programs join the list as research demands.

Plate I, Active Broadnose Sevengill Notorynchus cepedianus

A coastal predator with seven gill slits and a dense pattern of freckles whose long-term stability we are actively studying. Found across temperate coastal waters of the Pacific, Atlantic, and Indian Oceans. The flagship.

§ III Why It Matters
OSEA Science is a California marine-conservation nonprofit organized as a 501(c)(3). Donations support student researchers, volunteer divers, and the day-to-day operations that keep the program running.

A photograph is a small thing. A catalogue is a different animal.

Knowing how many sevengills there are, where they go, and how long they live changes how we manage the bays they call home. It changes fishing rules, protected-area policy, and our basic understanding of the species. The catalogue is the only way to find out, and citizen submissions are the only way to scale it.
