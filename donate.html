§ I Where the money goes

Compute. The catalogue runs computer-vision models to detect bodies, heads, and freckle patterns automatically. That's what makes citizen submissions tractable at scale, and it's most of our cost.

Fieldwork. Ground-truth data — known individuals photographed across multiple years — is what trains and validates the catalogue. OSEA staff and volunteers spend most of summer in the bays doing that work.

Outreach. Aquariums, dive shops, schools, and other partner organizations onboard onto the platform free of charge. We pay for their printed QR codes, signage, and basic training.

§ II Other ways to help

Submit a sighting. Photographs are the catalogue. A clean profile of a sevengill is worth hours of fieldwork.

Register your organization. If you run an aquarium, dive shop, school program, or research lab and can drive submissions or co-host the program, we'll set up a partner page in your name.

Spread the word. Most sevengill photographers don't know we exist. A link in a dive newsletter or a poster at the bait shop goes further than money.
